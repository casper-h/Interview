# Behavioural Preparation

Behavioural interviews test how you applied judgment through real work. Prepare evidence for ownership, conflict, ambiguity, failure, influence, prioritization, mentorship, incidents, cross-team work, strategy, and technical decisions. Use only true examples and remove confidential details that are not needed to explain the decision.

## Three Different Formats

| Format | Subject | Typical answer shape |
|---|---|---|
| Behavioural story | One situation that demonstrates a competency | A concise 90-second to 3-minute story followed by probing |
| [Experience deep dive](./experience_deep_dive.md) | A real system or project you helped build | A 30 to 60-minute technical and organizational investigation |
| Hypothetical system design | A new prompt with invented requirements | Requirements, high-level design (HLD), deep dives, and trade-offs |

Do not answer an experience deep dive as a long STAR story. Do not redesign a real project into an idealized greenfield system. Explain what actually existed, your role, the constraints, and what you would now change.

## Canonical Path

1. Use the [story guide](./story_guide.md) to inventory projects and draft four reusable stories. Expand to six to eight before active interviews.
2. Prepare the standard narrative pieces: tell me about yourself, two project walkthroughs, and why this company.
3. Build one [experience deep dive](./experience_deep_dive.md) from a system you know directly.
4. Practice concise initial answers. Ask a partner to probe decisions, disagreement, impact, alternatives, and hindsight.
5. Before a real loop, verify the current format with the recruiter and map stories to the company's published values.

Move to target-specific practice when four stories cover the main competencies and one deep dive survives 30 minutes of probing. Before active interviews, aim for six to eight flexible stories, one complete deep dive, and a second deep dive only when the loop or resume breadth makes it useful.

## Coverage

Your set of stories should collectively cover:

- end-to-end ownership and delivery;
- a substantive conflict and influence without authority;
- ambiguity and prioritization under constraints;
- a failure, wrong decision, or missed signal followed by a concrete change;
- mentorship, sponsorship, or raising the quality of a team;
- an incident and operational judgment under pressure;
- cross-team or cross-functional execution;
- technical strategy, including what not to build;
- a difficult technical trade-off with evidence and alternatives.

One rich project can cover several dimensions. Do not manufacture a separate story for every category.

## Interview-Specific Preparation

Company practices are volatile. Ask the recruiter about interview names, duration, expected scope, whether an experience deep dive is included, and whether interviewers want slides, diagrams, or code. Use current official company material where available. Treat anonymous community reports as anecdotes, not policy.

During the final preparation period:

- adapt story selection to the published values and role scope;
- prepare specific reasons for the company, product, and team;
- review the resume for any claim likely to trigger a deep dive;
- prepare questions about the team's goals, operating model, technical risks, and expectations for the role;
- rehearse naturally from bullet points rather than memorizing wording.
