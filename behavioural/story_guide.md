# Behavioural Story Guide

## Story Inventory

List the projects, incidents, decisions, conflicts, and people-development work you know well. Prefer recent examples with meaningful scope, but use the strongest truthful evidence available. Tag each candidate story with the competencies in the [behavioural overview](./README.md#coverage), then select six to eight stories that provide broad coverage.

## Build the Story

Use STAR as a compression tool, not a script.

- **Situation:** Give only the context needed to understand the stakes.
- **Task:** State your responsibility and why the problem mattered.
- **Action:** Spend most of the answer on your decisions, reasoning, communication, and execution. Distinguish your contribution from the team's.
- **Result:** Explain measurable impact, unintended effects, and what happened afterward.
- **Reflection:** State what you learned, what you would change, or what new evidence would have changed the decision.

Lead with a one-sentence headline when useful: “I will use an incident where I coordinated three teams to restore service and then changed our rollout controls.” A concise initial answer leaves time for the interviewer to choose the depth.

## Follow-Up Probes

Every story should survive these questions:

- Why did you choose that option?
- What was the strongest alternative?
- What did you personally own?
- Who disagreed, and what did you learn from them?
- What was the hardest trade-off?
- What went wrong or surprised you?
- How do you know the result was caused by your work?
- What would you do with half the time or budget?
- What would fail at ten times the scale?
- What did you change afterward?

Do not invent a disagreement, failure, or metric. State uncertainty honestly.

## Standard Narratives

### Tell Me About Yourself

Use 60 to 90 seconds:

1. Your current scope and specialty.
2. The career through-line that explains how you reached it.
3. One or two relevant outcomes.
4. What you want next and why it fits this role.

Do not narrate every job chronologically.

### Project Walkthrough

In about 90 seconds, explain the problem, scale, your role, the central technical or organizational decision, and the outcome. Prepare two projects. The interviewer can then choose one for deeper questions.

### Why This Company

Connect three concrete facts:

- a product, customer, or mission problem you genuinely care about;
- a technical or organizational challenge that fits your experience and growth goals;
- evidence from current company material, the role description, or conversations.

Avoid praise that applies to any employer. Verify current facts shortly before the interview.

## Practice

Tell each story once in two minutes and once in five minutes. Ask a partner to interrupt, change direction, and challenge the result. Revise content that is confusing, but do not memorize sentences. Keep filled templates in private notes when they contain employer or customer information.
