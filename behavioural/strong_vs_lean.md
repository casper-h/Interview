# Strong Hire Versus Lean Hire, on the Staff-Scope Rows

Rows 1 to 10 of the [story bank](./README.md) cover the individual-contributor baseline; rows 11 to
20 are the scope, influence, and leadership stories that push an evaluation from lean hire to strong
hire. For each of those rows, below is what a strong-hire answer demonstrates concretely, what the
lean-hire version sounds like, and the single follow-up probe that separates them. Use this to
pressure-test your own stories: if your draft sounds like the lean-hire column, it is not ready.

**Row 11, driving a technical change across multiple teams.** Strong hire: you name the teams, the
objection each raised, and how you addressed their cost or timeline concern rather than escalating
over their heads. Lean hire: "we rolled it out," with no named resistance and no account of how you
influenced without authority. Probe: "Which team pushed back hardest, and why?"

**Row 12, owning a multi-quarter technical direction.** Strong hire: you set a strategy, revisited it
mid-flight as the context changed, and defended the pivot with evidence. Lean hire: you executed a
roadmap someone else defined. Probe: "What would you change if you ran it again, and what did you get
wrong?"

**Row 13, expanding the scope unprompted.** Strong hire: you found a problem no one asked you to
solve, justified the wider scope with impact or cost-avoidance data, and delivered it. Lean hire: "I
went above and beyond," with no clear why and no quantified outcome. Probe: "Who disagreed with the
expanded scope, and what did they say?"

**Row 14, raising the bar for others.** Strong hire: you name the specific standard that was slipping
(test coverage, design-review rigor, incident response), the resistance you met, and the measurable
change afterward. Lean hire: "I mentored someone," with no outcome. Probe: "What did the team look
like six months later, and how do you know the bar stayed raised?"

**Row 15, hard feedback given or received.** Strong hire: you narrate a specific, uncomfortable
conversation, what you said, how the other person reacted, and what changed. Lean hire: vague ("I told
them to improve") or defensive. Probe: "What exact words did you use, and what would you say
differently now?"

**Row 16, non-code work that unblocked the team.** Strong hire: you quantify the time saved, name the
process you built or the docs you wrote, and explain why no one else picked it up. Lean hire: "I helped
with onboarding," with no measurable impact. Probe: "How many people did this affect, and what broke
when it was missing?"

**Row 17, cross-org disagreement with no authority.** Strong hire: you name both orgs, the substance
of the conflict, and the data or compromise you brokered that let both move forward. Lean hire: "I
facilitated alignment," with no detail. Probe: "What did each side want, and which one gave ground?"

**Row 18, defining the problem, not just solving it.** Strong hire: you reshaped the handed-down plan,
named the real bottleneck, and delivered against your redefinition. Lean hire: you executed the brief
as given. Probe: "Who disagreed with your reframe, and what was their version of the problem?"

**Row 19, inheriting a troubled project.** Strong hire: you diagnose what was broken (unclear scope,
wrong team, wrong technology), the first three changes you made, and the outcome. Lean hire: "I turned
it around," with no root-cause account. Probe: "What would have happened if you had not joined, and how
do you know?"

**Row 20, a quality-versus-speed call.** Strong hire: you name the known debt you shipped with, or the
feature you cut to hold the line, defend it with a cost or risk model, and say whether hindsight proved
you right. Lean hire: "we balanced both," with no real tension. Probe: "Who wanted the opposite call,
and what was their argument?"

These map onto the frameworks companies grade against. Rows 11, 13, 18, and 20 are Ownership and Bias
for Action in the [Amazon Leadership Principles](https://www.amazon.jobs/content/en/our-workplace/leadership-principles);
row 15 is Earn Trust and Have Backbone; row 14 is Hire and Develop the Best. The leadership-without-
authority rows (11, 17) and the intellectual-humility rows (15, 18, 20) are the same signals
[Google re:Work](https://rework.withgoogle.com/) documents in its hiring and manager research. Tag
each of your stories with the rows and principles it serves, so you can reach for the right one in the
moment rather than searching for it live.
